# Proof case: Crown & Core tenant demo

Audited URL: `https://tenantdemo.thehelpfulai.cloud/`

Audit date: 2026-09-30.

This is a proof-of-method audit, not a claim about who wrote the site. Origin is unknown.

## Human job

A prospective Crown & Core client should be able to:

1. understand what Crown & Core is;
2. identify whether a current service/offer is relevant;
3. trust the provider, process and claims enough to continue;
4. understand what happens next;
5. book or request the appropriate consultation with low friction.

## What survives

**KEEP VERBATIM / KEEP AND REPOSITION**

- Bellevue location, daily hours, phone and address.
- Real facility photography.
- Four service categories as secondary navigation.
- Direct booking path.
- Concrete treatment pages such as T-Shape 2 that explain mechanism, session duration, areas, sequence and expected visit flow.
- Privacy / health-data language and the instruction not to submit detailed medical history through the booking form.

## Homepage slop map

### `Wellness, elevated.`
**VERDICT:** REMOVE.

Why: portable category language. It does not identify a service, audience, mechanism, credential or next step.

What is lost if removed: no required information.

### `A modern wellness destination ... personalized care, beauty, recovery, and performance.`
**VERDICT:** REDUCE.

Why: broad positioning repeats what the service taxonomy already says.

Action: one plain sentence that establishes location + what the place combines, then move into the current commercial offer.

### `One destination, four disciplines.` + four “service worlds” + eight “signature experiences”
**VERDICT:** KEEP CATEGORY NAVIGATION; REMOVE ACQUISITION EQUALITY.

Why: the homepage asks the visitor to process the whole catalogue before knowing what Crown & Core currently wants them to do.

Action: keep a compact service directory, but let one current hero offer own the acquisition story.

### `Designed to feel different`
**VERDICT:** REMOVE.

Why: asserts difference rather than showing it. The photography already demonstrates the space.

### `Personalized Care / One Destination / Advanced Modalities / Elevated Experience`
**VERDICT:** REDUCE OR REPLACE WITH EVIDENCE.

Why: these are category-level claims. “Advanced” and “elevated” need proof; “personalized” needs an observable process.

Action: show provider credentials, consultation process, device names, treatment plan mechanics or other verifiable facts.

### Anonymous guest quote
**VERDICT:** REPLACE WITH EVIDENCE unless provenance is verified.

Why: the page shows a quote and service type but no source identity or verification context. The Extractor does not call it fake; it treats provenance as unknown.

## Booking flow

The booking form contains a very large service selector spanning medical wellness, skin/body, salon and recovery. This is a cognitive-load problem even if the list is accurate.

**R1 proposal:**
- If arriving from an offer page, preselect the service.
- Otherwise ask first what the visitor wants help with, then reveal the relevant service choices.
- Keep “Not sure — consultation” as an escape hatch.
- Use one consistent primary CTA label.

## T-Shape 2 page

This page is materially stronger because it contains concrete information:
- 30–60 minute session guidance;
- no-downtime claim;
- named treatment areas;
- treatment sequence;
- typical series guidance;
- four technology mechanisms.

The commercial rebuild should use that specificity as the acquisition spine.

### Evidence gate
Claims such as FDA clearance, fat-volume reduction, collagen stimulation, cellulite reduction and lymphatic effects require source/compliance review before paid campaigns or stronger medical claims. The Extractor flags the evidence requirement; it does not independently certify the claims.

## R1 — essential homepage

1. Location + what Crown & Core is.
2. One current hero offer.
3. Who it is for / who it is not for.
4. What happens during the service.
5. Real proof: provider, device/process, verified reviews/results where allowed.
6. One booking action.
7. Compact “other services” directory.
8. Address / hours / phone / legal.

## R2 — human

Write in specific service language. Avoid “elevated,” “experience,” and “thoughtfully designed” unless they communicate something concrete.

## R3 — composed

`offer → proof → what happens → questions/objections → booking → other services`

## R4 — minimum useful

Remove each remaining decorative or repeated section one at a time. Stop when a removal makes the visitor less able to understand fit, trust the service, or book safely.
